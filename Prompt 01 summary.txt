## 🤖 AI Advocate Briefing: Lessons from Deloitte on Using AI for Real Business Growth

*Based on an AI News interview with Prakul Sharma, Principal & AI Practice Leader, Deloitte Consulting*

---

### 📖 Key Terms — Plain English

**AI (Artificial Intelligence)** — Computer systems that think, analyze, and make decisions like humans.

**GenAI / Generative AI** — AI that *creates* content — emails, summaries, answers. Think ChatGPT. Useful, but limited to productivity tasks.

**Agentic AI / AI Agents** — AI that goes beyond answering questions to actually *taking action* — completing multi-step tasks, navigating systems, executing decisions on your behalf.

**Autonomous Intelligence** — The most advanced stage, where AI independently makes and carries out decisions within boundaries your organization sets.

**ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning)** — Core business software managing finance, purchasing, inventory, and HR (e.g., SAP, Oracle).

**Decision-Grade Data** — Real-time, traceable data reliable enough for AI to act on. Opposite of reporting-grade data sitting in weekly dashboards.

**Hallucination** — When AI confidently gives a wrong or made-up answer — a serious business risk.

**RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation)** — A technique that reduces hallucinations by grounding AI responses in your company's actual data.

**Governance / Governance Debt** — The rules and oversight controlling AI behavior. "Debt" is what builds up when those rules are skipped during testing.

**Human-in-the-Loop** — A safety design where humans must approve certain AI decisions before action is taken.

**API (Application Programming Interface)** — The technical bridge connecting different software systems.

**Pilot** — A small-scale test of a new tool or process before company-wide rollout.

---

### 🧭 The Big Picture: Why Basic AI Isn't Enough

Generating text or summarizing internal communications offers only localized productivity improvements and rarely changes the core cost or revenue structure of a large organization. Deloitte's message is direct: chatbots and document summaries are a starting point, not a destination. The real competitive advantage comes from AI that can *act* — not just assist. Businesses that stay at the "helpful assistant" stage of AI will fall behind those deploying systems that remove bottlenecks, cut costs, and drive revenue without requiring a human to manage every step.

---

### 📈 Section 1: The Three Stages of AI Maturity

Deloitte views AI adoption as a three-stage curve: "assisted intelligence" (AI helping people interpret information), "artificial intelligence" (machine learning augmenting human decisions), and "autonomous intelligence" (AI deciding and executing within defined boundaries). Today's GenAI tools sit in the middle of that curve, while agentic AI acts as the bridge into full autonomy — and that is where the center of gravity is shifting now.

**Our takeaway:** Most of us are at Stage 1 or 2. The goal is Stage 3 — where AI doesn't just inform us, it *works* for us.

---

### 🔍 Section 2: Start with a "Decision Audit"

Sharma advises leaders to pick one or two value chains where outcomes are bottlenecked by decisions — not tasks — and map how those decisions are made today: who has the data, who has the authority, where handoffs break, and where judgment is being applied. This surfaces workflows where autonomy creates real economic value while exposing data and governance gaps.

**Our takeaway:** Before buying any tool, map where slow decisions are costing us time and money. Those bottlenecks are our best AI opportunities.

---

### 🗄️ Section 3: Your Data Has to Be Ready

The model is rarely the bottleneck — frontier AI capability is becoming a commodity. Where enterprises trip up is upstream: selecting a use case before mapping the workflow, and underestimating that autonomous systems need decision-grade data — with lineage and access controls — not the reporting-grade data most enterprise systems were built to provide.

**Our takeaway:** Weekly batch reports and siloed spreadsheets won't cut it for AI agents. Real-time, traceable, trustworthy data is the foundation everything else is built on.

---

### 💰 Section 4: Plan for the Cost of Scale

Because agentic workflows involve multiple AI interactions to reason through a single goal, API costs can escalate unpredictably. Reducing hallucination risks through retrieval-augmented generation also increases compute overhead, requiring strict financial controls before enterprise deployment.

**Our takeaway:** AI at scale isn't free. Every workflow an AI agent runs involves multiple system calls that add up fast. We need a cost model before we roll out broadly — not after the bills arrive.

---

### 🏛️ Section 5: Avoid the "Production Gap" and Governance Debt

The "production gap" is the main roadblock: a pilot can succeed with a curated dataset and a champion team, but enterprise deployment requires continuous evaluations, proper identity and authorization across all systems, change management for users, and a financial model that absorbs usage-based costs. Governance debt — controls and compliance frameworks skipped to accelerate a pilot — becomes the gating item when legal reviews a production rollout.

**Our takeaway:** Don't bypass security and compliance to get a quick win. Those shortcuts become the exact reason the tool never scales.

---

### 🔁 Section 6: Build Once, Scale Many Times

The clients that break through treat pilots not as experiments but as the first production instance of a reusable platform — with the same evaluations, identity model, and governance built in from day one. This allows the second and third use cases to build on the first instead of starting over.

**Our takeaway:** Every AI pilot should be engineered to grow. Involve IT, legal, and compliance from the start — so each new use case becomes faster and cheaper to deploy, not another rebuild from scratch.

---

### ✅ Our Action Plan — 5 Things We Should Do Now

1. **Run a decision audit** — identify where slow decisions or manual approvals are costing us speed or money.
2. **Assess our data quality** — determine if our data is real-time and traceable enough for AI to act on reliably.
3. **Start small, but build to scale** — every pilot should include governance, security, and compliance from day one.
4. **Budget for AI compute costs** — factor in the true ongoing cost of agentic workflows before committing.
5. **Keep humans in the loop** — set clear approval thresholds so AI acts confidently on routine decisions and escalates the rest.

The companies winning with AI aren't the ones with the most tools. They're the ones with the clearest *process* — and that process starts today.
